# MUSIC_NOTES.md — the score for *Geeked Geese*

> *"Soundtrack: Canadiana through a blown speaker. Fiddle, but wrong."*
> — GEEKED_GEESE_DESIGN.md §8

Everything here is generated by `tools/gen_music.py` (numpy + stdlib `wave`,
no samples, no downloads) and mixed at runtime by `scripts/core/music.gd`
(autoload `Music`).

## 1. Research, and what it changed

**Vertical layering vs. horizontal re-sequencing.** The Game Audio Co's
comparison lays out the trade cleanly: vertical layering suits "real-time
intensity shifts" in unpredictable play — combat, exploration, open worlds —
and gives smooth blending at the cost of memory and mixing complexity;
horizontal re-sequencing suits "story-driven or segmented gameplay" and risks
"potentially jarring transitions" plus "less flexibility during fast-changing
gameplay." A goose destroying a parking lot is about as unpredictable as
gameplay gets, so the moment-to-moment score is **vertical**. The recommended
hybrid — horizontal between phases, vertical inside them — is what we do:
zone → migration → zone are hard scene swaps, so those get a crossfade, and
everything inside a leg is volume automation.
([thegameaudioco.com](https://www.thegameaudioco.com/making-your-game-s-music-more-dynamic-vertical-layering-vs-horizontal-resequencing))

**Why loops become irritating.** Gamedeveloper.com's piece on rethinking the
audio loop is blunt about the mechanism: players "quickly identify the looping
point," short loops "struggle to incorporate musical contrast," and — the part
that actually changed the composition — *loop-safe writing backfires on
itself*: when a composer raises the key or adds instruments to build
intensity, "the loop resets this progression, creating deflation." Its
prescriptions: reorder parts, mute the melody selectively, use strategic
silence (Halo's trick of going quiet after ~3 repetitions), and write
expandable endings.
([gamedeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/rethinking-the-audio-loop-in-games))

Four things came directly out of that:

- Loops are **16 bars / 45.7 s**, not 8 bars, with a real A / B / turnaround
  shape inside them, so the loop point is a bar line rather than a rewind.
- **Selective melody muting happens *inside* the loop**: bar 12 of the zone
  tune has no melody at all, and bar 8 of the migration tune is a full bar of
  rest. The listener's brain gets a gap before it gets bored.
- The build-then-deflate trap is dodged by never building *within* the file.
  The arrangement grows by **layers the game adds**, which don't reset at the
  loop point because the loop point isn't where they came from.
- **Strategic silence is wired to a game event, not a timer**: after a
  blackout the melody stays gone for eight seconds. Free, motivated, and it
  resets loop fatigue every time the player mixes three substances.

**Scoring comedy.** NPR's piece on straight-faced comedy scoring is the
governing rule for this whole score. Theodore Shapiro: *"If you get to one
place and then you say, 'Well, let's just do something really silly here so we
can play the comedy and give the audience permission to laugh'... it only
really takes one or two moments of breaking the spell to ruin the atmosphere
you're creating."* Charles Fox names the failure mode as trying to write
"what they used to call 'comedic music'."
([npr.org](https://www.npr.org/2016/07/16/486060013/what-makes-a-comedy-funnier-music-with-a-straight-face))

So: **there is no joke in the music.** No slide whistle, no wah-wah, no
pizzicato "sneaking" cue, no kazoo. The band is playing a real, slightly sad
folk tune in a parking lot and has not been told what you are doing. The one
technique borrowed from the games-comedy side (gamedeveloper.com's *Comedic
Sound for the Game Music Composer*: thwarted expectation) is applied to
**timbre, not gesture** — the mains hum is in the harmony and is a quarter-tone
out of any key; the lawnmower is a tuned drone; the fiddle is 25 cents flat on
four notes out of forty. Those aren't jokes, they're a *place*.
([gamedeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/comedic-sound-for-the-game-music-composer))

**Stingers.** Standard practice (Berklee Online / *Writing Interactive Music
for Video Games* ch. 10) is that a stinger must belong to the cue it lands on
— same key, same tempo, same instruments — or it reads as a UI notification.
All six stingers here are 84 BPM, D mixolydian, and played by the same five
instruments as the bed.
([online.berklee.edu](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/scoring-for-games-top-techniques-for-composing-music-for-interactive-media/),
[oreilly.com](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/writing-interactive-music/9780133563528/ch10.html))

**Ducking.** Gamedeveloper.com's *Game Audio Theory: Ducking* gives the shape:
bus-based (not sidechain — sidechain "doesn't work for interactive media"),
about **−9 dB, 500 ms attack, 1000 ms release** for dialogue. We use a
shallower −4 to −6 dB with a **much faster ~60 ms attack**, because we're
ducking under transient SFX and stingers rather than under speech, and a slow
attack on a transient is audible as a swell — worse than not ducking. Its
warning that "overuse becomes distracting" is why only four things duck.
([gamedeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/game-audio-theory-ducking))

## 2. Architecture

**Vertical remixing, with horizontal movement only at scene swaps.**

All 16 bed stems load at boot, `play()` in one loop inside `_start_all()`, and
are **never stopped**. Only volumes move. This is chosen because it makes
synchronisation a structural property rather than a scheduling problem:

| requirement | how it's met |
|---|---|
| same tempo | every stem is 84 BPM |
| same key | every stem is D mixolydian |
| same length | every stem is **exactly 1,008,000 samples** (16 bars) |
| same start | all `play()` calls in one loop → one audio-server tick |
| same rate | pitch is written to every player in the same frame |

Same length + same start + same rate = permanently phase-locked. A layer can
be introduced mid-bar with no transition asset, because it was already
playing — it was just at −80 dB. There is a resync watchdog every 5 s as
insurance, and it only ever corrects players that are currently **inaudible**,
so the correction itself can never be heard.

Horizontal re-sequencing was rejected for the main loop because it requires a
bar clock, transition stingers, and a "switch at the next downbeat" queue —
three subsystems, each of which fails audibly. It survives only where the game
already hard-cuts (zone ↔ migration), where a 1.5 s crossfade between two cues
that share a key and a tempo is a modulation, not a seam.

### Bus graph (built at runtime, no `.tres` bus layout)

```
Master
 └── Music          ← volume owned by Prefs (`music_volume`); Music owns only its mute flag
      ├── MusicBed     ← the 16 loops. LowPassFilter + PitchShift live here. This ducks.
      └── MusicSting   ← the 6 one-shots. Never ducked, never filtered.
```

The pitch shifter is present but **disabled by default** — Godot's is a phase
vocoder and smears transients even at ratio 1.0, and this score is mostly
transients. It's switched on only while a substance needs it, at which point
the smear is a feature.

### Loop-seam technique (the part that took the longest)

Everything in the generator is **circular by construction**, which is what
makes the seams exact rather than crossfaded:

- `wrap_add()` — notes are mixed modulo the loop length, so a bass note struck
  at bar 16 beat 4 continues its tail into bar 1. The loop point stops being
  an edit and becomes a bar line.
- **All filtering is FFT-domain.** An FFT over exactly one loop length treats
  the buffer as periodic, so every EQ move, filter and reverb is circular.
  (`circ_reverb` is a circular convolution — the last chord's tail genuinely
  wraps into the first.)
- `periodic_noise()` — tape hiss built from a random-phase flat spectrum, so
  it is exactly periodic at any amplitude.
- `wow_flutter()` — resampling on LFOs with **integer cycle counts** (3 / 29 /
  181 per loop). Deliberately not multiples of 16 bars, so the pitch
  instability drifts against the bar grid and never lands the same way twice
  within one pass. It's an anti-repetition device disguised as a broken
  cassette.
- `snap_hz()` — the three continuous drones (60 Hz mains hum, the mower, the
  lake sub) are snapped to the nearest frequency completing a whole number of
  cycles in the loop. 60 Hz over 45.714 s is 2742.857 cycles; snapping moves
  it by 0.011 Hz and removes a step that was otherwise there on every pass.
  **This was a real bug found by measurement, not theory.**
- `seam_null()` — finally, the residual jump is driven to exact zero by
  spreading the correction over 4096 samples (186 ms) at each edge. `gen_audio.py`
  does this over 16 samples; on a dark sustained pad a 16-sample correction is
  *itself* a ~1 kHz transient. 4096 samples puts the correction's entire
  spectrum below ~5 Hz.

### Verified numbers (from `python3 tools/gen_music.py`)

```
worst seam/local-slope ratio  : 0.00    (<= 3.0  = no waveform step)
worst doubled-loop tick       : -1.74 dB vs the stem's own 99.9th pct transient
ALL LOOPS SEAMLESS.

FULL-LAYER SUM (all four layers of a zone at intensity 1.0)
  strip_mall   peak -3.00 dBFS  RMS -18.09 dBFS  clipped samples: 0
  suburbia     peak -3.00 dBFS  RMS -18.38 dBFS  clipped samples: 0
  cottage      peak -3.00 dBFS  RMS -16.55 dBFS  clipped samples: 0
  migration    peak -3.00 dBFS  RMS -16.54 dBFS  clipped samples: 0
```

Two independent seam tests, both reported per file:
1. **Slope ratio** — the wrap step vs. the typical sample-to-sample slope
   either side. Now 0.00 everywhere because `seam_null()` makes the step
   exactly zero.
2. **Doubled-loop tick** — concatenate the stem with itself (literally what
   the engine does), highpass at 6 kHz, take a short-time peak envelope, and
   ask whether the block containing the seam is an outlier against every other
   block. Worst case is **−1.74 dB**, i.e. the seam isn't even in the top 0.1%
   of transients the stem already contains.

Levels are **not** per-stem normalised — that would make the shaker as loud as
the bass and destroy the arrangement. Each zone is scaled by **one common
factor** so its full four-layer sum peaks at −3 dBFS. The mix balance is
composed; only the headroom is measured.

### Compositional shape (why 45 s doesn't feel like 8 s repeated)

```
bars  1– 8   A   D  D  C  G  |  D  D  C  G      the tune, then restated an octave up
bars  9–14   B   Bm Bm G  A  |  Bm G            minor; MELODY ABSENT for bar 12
bars 15–16   T   C  →  Asus4                    no third, no cadence, back to D
```

The bVII (C natural) never resolves in a zone loop — bar 16 is an Asus4 with
no third at all, and the single C♯ in 45 seconds is a passing tone in bar 12.
**The migration set is where the C becomes C♯ and the V–I lands** (bar 12 is a
real A7, bar 13 is D). Two zones of being denied a cadence is what makes the
flight feel like the only sincere thing in the game — without a key change, a
tempo change, or a single "epic" gesture.

Measured evidence that the loops go somewhere: lead layers have a **49–61 dB**
RMS range across the loop (the rests are real), rhythm layers 8.7–55 dB, and
first-half vs second-half energy differs by up to 9.2 dB where the arrangement
is designed to bloom. The two genuinely flat stems are the pads (4.6–8.2 dB)
and `mus_mig_pulse` (3.5 dB) — a steady pulse *should* be steady; its variation
is in pitch content (centroid 1413→2089 Hz across four different bass figures)
rather than in level.

## 4. Adaptive rules — exactly what changes the music

`intensity` (0…1) is the single scalar the layer mix runs on:

```
intensity = clamp( max(combo/8, heat, zone_floor) + adrenaline_push , 0, 1 )
            then max(…, whatever set_intensity() asked for)
```

- **`combo/8`** — `Game.combo`, read every frame.
- **`heat`** — bumped to `0.18 + 0.06·combo` by every `Game.chaos_scored`,
  decaying with τ = 3.6 s. Lets a single big hit lift the mix briefly.
- **`zone_floor`** — 0.30 strip mall / 0.30 suburbia / 0.26 cottage /
  **0.55 migration**. The mix never drops below this. Without a floor the
  backbeat appears when you hit something and evaporates six seconds later,
  and the score reads as a reactive system rather than as a band already in
  the room. What responds to the player is the *tune* and the *top end*, not
  whether music is happening at all.
- **`adrenaline_push`** — `0.42 · clamp((adrenaline−0.45)/0.55)`, **added** on
  top so being wired while idle and being wired mid-chain sound different.
- **`set_intensity(v)`** sets a **floor, not an absolute**: the final value is
  `max(what you asked for, what the game is asking for)`. Sticky (say it once,
  not every frame) and impossible to use to accidentally disable the adaptive
  behaviour. `set_intensity(0.0)` hands control back entirely.

Layer entry windows (smoothstep):

| role | zones | migration |
|---|---|---|
| base | always on | always on |
| rhythm | 0.04 → 0.34 | 0.02 → 0.26 |
| lead | 0.22 → 0.58 | **0.08 → 0.34** |
| hi | 0.55 → 0.92 | 0.40 → 0.82 |

Fades: 0.85 s in, 1.55 s out (layers should ebb, not cut) — except a
deliberately killed melody, which gets 0.35 s so it reads as "the tune
stopped" rather than as the mix losing interest.

## 5. What substances do to the mix

Everything below reads `Subs.mods` / `Subs.brain` and is smoothed, never
stepped.

### Nitrous and `Engine.time_scale` — the decision, and why

**The music follows, but on a compressed curve, and it reads the *intent*, not
the clock.**

Godot's audio thread ignores `Engine.time_scale`, so *doing nothing* is a
decision too: it means the music **resists** — the world goes to syrup and the
band plays on at full speed. We reject that. The entire identity of this score
is a cassette in a cold car, and a tape transport slowing down is the most
legible sound in the world. If the picture drags and the tape doesn't, the gag
has no soundtrack.

But following it *linearly* is worse. `time_scale = 0.45` mapped straight onto
playback rate is more than an octave down: the fiddle becomes a foghorn, 84 BPM
becomes 38 BPM, and every layer carefully placed in its own frequency band
lands on top of the bass. Funny once. Unbearable by leg two — and this is a
14-second effect the player will trigger dozens of times per run.

So:

```gdscript
pitch = clamp(pow(time_scale, 0.45), 0.72, 1.10)
```

Nitrous lands on the **0.72 floor — a minor third down**: unmistakably a
transport dragging, still musical, still countable in 4. Slew is asymmetric
(0.30 s down, 0.85 s up) so it spools like a motor rather than cutting.

**Critically, it reads `Subs.mods.time_scale` — the substance system's
intent — never `Engine.time_scale` itself.** `juice.gd` dips
`Engine.time_scale` for hitstop on *every impact*; a score that warbles each
time you clip a shopping cart is not a broken tape, it's a broken score. This
is the same rule `juice.gd` already states for itself, for the same reason,
and the harness asserts it explicitly (`_test_hitstop_immunity`).

Because the pitch is written to **every bed player in the same frame**, tape
speed cannot desync the layers.

### The rest

| substance signal | what the mix does | why |
|---|---|---|
| **GABA** high (bars, gravy, grass) | lowpass 20 kHz → 2.4 kHz | GABA *is* the "everything is soft and far away" axis; a lowpass is literally that |
| **Dopamine** high | cutoff × up to 1.5 | chemical brightness rendered as actual brightness |
| **Adrenaline** high | `+0.42` intensity | caffeine and nitrous make the arrangement busier standing still, which is what they do to you |
| **Comedown** | cutoff ≤ 2.6 kHz, bed −2 dB | the only effect the player never chose, so it's the only purely subtractive one — no wobble, no sparkle, just less |
| **`fx_mode == 3`** (slow-mo) | cutoff ≤ 3.6 kHz | a dragging tape loses its top end, always has |
| **`scale_amp`** (fly agaric / macropsia) | pitch-shift effect follows the **same LFO the goose's size uses**: `2^(−0.42·amp·sin(2πt/period))` | as you grow the score drops, as you shrink it rises. Your size and the music's register become the same variable. Costs one `sin()`. |
| **`hallucinate`** (deliriants) | ±3.5% wandering detune from two incommensurate sines | nothing about a deliriant settles, so neither does the tuning; two sines so it doesn't read as vibrato |
| **`hud_amnesia`** (benzos) | **the lead layer goes to zero** | benzos wipe the HUD. The tune is the melodic HUD — it's what tells you where you are in the bar. So it goes too, and you're left with a bed, a backbeat, and no idea how long you've been here. Played completely straight, which is why it works. |

### Blackout — the memorable one

Three seconds, and it's allowed to be extreme *because* it's three seconds and
because it's the loudest structural event in the game.

**On `blackout_started`:**
- bed bus collapses to **−34 dB** (not a fade — a collapse)
- lowpass **slams to 180 Hz** in ~120 ms
- transport drags to **0.55×**
- `mus_sting_blackout` fires on the un-ducked sting bus: tape stop, two reel
  flaps against the head, a dead thud, hiss

What you're left with is the low thud of music happening in another room,
which is roughly what passing out actually sounds like.

**On `blackout_ended` — the half that makes it memorable:**
- `mus_sting_wake` — the transport spooling back up
- the bed climbs back over **2.6 s** with the filter sweeping 300 Hz → open
  behind it, so waking up is a gesture rather than a cut
- **the melody stays gone for a further 8 seconds.** This is the
  gamedeveloper.com "strategic silence" technique, wired to a game event
  instead of a repeat counter: it costs nothing, it's diegetically motivated,
  and every blackout resets the player's loop fatigue for free.

### Required (2 minutes, and this is genuinely all of it)

**1. Generate the audio.**

```bash
cd tools && python3 gen_music.py
```

Writes 22 `.wav` + 22 `.wav.import` into `audio/` (32.8 MB, ~30 s to run) and
prints the stem table, the seam report, the A-weighted band table, the
per-loop variation table and the clip check.

The `.import` files are **shipped deliberately**. They set `compress/mode=0`
(PCM) and `edit/loop_mode=1` (forward). Godot's default for `.wav` is QOA,
which is lossy and resets its predictor every frame — on a loop whose seam is
sample-exact, that's a coin flip on an audible tick every 45 seconds. The
harness asserts the imported resource really is `FORMAT_16_BITS` and
`LOOP_FORWARD`.

**2. Drop in the script.**

```
scripts/core/music.gd
```

**3. Register the autoload — LAST, after `Mission`.** In `project.godot`:

```ini
[autoload]

Prefs="*res://scripts/core/prefs.gd"
Tex="*res://scripts/core/tex.gd"
Sfx="*res://scripts/core/sfx.gd"
Subs="*res://scripts/core/substance_manager.gd"
Game="*res://scripts/core/game.gd"
Flock="*res://scripts/core/flock_state.gd"
Unlocks="*res://scripts/core/unlocks.gd"
Juice="*res://scripts/core/juice.gd"
Mission="*res://scripts/core/mission.gd"
; Music last: it resolves Game/Subs/Mission/Prefs in _ready() and connects to
; their signals. It degrades to silence if any of them (or the wavs) are absent.
Music="*res://scripts/core/music.gd"
```

**That's it.** No change to `prefs.gd` (`music_volume` already exists in
`DEFAULTS` and `_apply_audio()` already looks up a bus named `Music` — which
`Music` now creates), no change to `pause_menu.gd` (the *Music volume* slider
row already exists at line 323), no change to `main.gd`, `game.gd`,
`mission.gd` or `substance_manager.gd`. The zone is picked up from
`Mission.objectives_ready`, the phase from `Game.phase_changed`, and the flight
cue from the appearance of the node named `Migration`. **Verified**: booting
the unmodified game headless with only the autoload line added produces
`zone=strip_mall` with the bed faded in by frame 60, and zero errors.

## 7. Honest assessment

**What is definitely true**, because it's measured rather than believed:

- All 16 loops have an edge discontinuity of **exactly 0.0** and the seam is
  not in the top 0.1% of each stem's own transients. They will not click.
- Nothing clips: full four-layer sums peak at −3.00 dBFS, 0 clipped samples.
- Layers occupy distinct A-weighted bands: base → mid, lead → upmid, hi →
  presence, rhythm → bass. They will not turn to mud when stacked.
- The loops contain real internal contrast (49–61 dB of RMS range in the lead
  layers, an absent-melody bar, four different bass figures in the migration
  pulse). They are not 8-second loops wearing a trenchcoat.
- The runtime behaves: 89/89 assertions, the real game boots clean, and it
  degrades to a single warning and silence with the WAVs deleted.

**What I cannot verify, and you should listen for:**

1. **I have never heard any of this.** Every judgement about *timbre* is
   inference from spectra. The most likely failure is the **accordion**: four
   detuned pulse waves through a lowpass is the right recipe, but "wheezing
   harmonium" and "cheap 1980s synth brass" are neighbours in synthesis space
   and only an ear can tell you which side it landed on. If it sounds like a
   synth pad, the fix is to raise `wheeze` and drop `bright` in `pad_layer()`.
2. **The fiddle is the whole brief and the biggest risk.** Five detuned saws +
   bow noise + three body formants is a defensible model, but real bowed
   strings have a bow-force/scratch character that additive synthesis does not
   naturally produce. It may read as "detuned saw lead" rather than "fiddle."
   The `bow` parameter is the lever — the "wrong" notes push it to 1.6, and if
   the whole thing sounds too clean, raising the global bow-noise level from
   `0.30` is the first thing to try.
3. **Loudness balance between layers is composed, not heard.** The relative
   levels come from typical arrangement practice and the A-weighted band
   table, not from listening. The specific number I'd bet against is the
   **`hi` layer**, which I raised by ~3 dB once already on measurement and
   which may still be too quiet to make maxing out a combo feel like anything.
4. **The zone floors (0.30 / 0.30 / 0.26) are a judgement call.** Too high and
   the adaptive system is inaudible; too low and the drums flicker on and off.
   0.30 keeps the backbeat permanently in and the lead at a whisper, which I
   believe is right, but it is exactly the sort of thing that's obvious in
   thirty seconds of play and unknowable from a spectrogram.
5. **The mall `rhythm` and `hi` layers both peak in the presence band.** I
   narrowed the backbeat brush to 3.6 kHz to separate them and they still
   share it. I decided this is *correct* — a snare and a hat share that region
   in every drum kit ever recorded — but if the busy mix sounds white and
   hashy, that's where it's coming from.
6. **32.8 MB of PCM** is a real cost for a game whose entire asset pipeline is
   procedural. It's the price of 45.7-second loops that don't feel like
   8-second ones, plus refusing QOA to protect the seams. Halving the loop to
   8 bars would halve the size and undo the single most important thing about
   the composition; I'd rather pay.
7. **Two engine warnings at shutdown** (`ObjectDB instances were leaked` /
   `resources still in use`). Reproduced on the *stock* project with a single
   looping `Sfx.loop()` call — it's 2 objects + 1 resource per playing looped
   stream in headless Godot 4.7.1, and Music has 16 of them. `_exit_tree()`
   stops every player and clears the stream cache and the count still doesn't
   reach zero, so it's an engine shutdown-ordering artefact, not a runtime
   leak. It has no effect on the running game, but it is not nothing and I
   didn't fix it.
8. **`AudioEffectPitchShift` quality is unknown to me.** It's off except under
   fly agaric and deliriants, so worst case is that two substances sound
   grainier than intended — which is arguably the correct outcome anyway.
9. **The migration cue carries the most weight and has the least insurance.**
   The whole emotional argument rests on one thing: that forty-five seconds of
   an unresolved bVII, twice, makes a plain V–I feel like an arrival. That's a
   real and well-understood effect, and it is also the kind of thing that
   works on paper and lands flat in practice. If it doesn't land, it will be
   because the migration arrangement is too similar in *texture* to the zone
   beds — same five instruments, same tape chain, just less damaged. The lever
   is `blown_speaker(wow=…)`: migration is at 0.35 vs 1.0 for the zones, and
   pushing it to 0.15 would make the flight noticeably cleaner and the contrast
   sharper.

The honest summary: I'm confident this is **structurally** good music-for-a-game
— it loops properly, it layers cleanly, it responds to the right things, and
it doesn't tell the joke. I'm moderately confident it's *pleasant*. I'm least
confident that the fiddle sounds like a fiddle, which is unfortunately the one
thing the design bible actually asked for.
